Prologue

The Night Everything Changed

I. The Live

It's almost eleven, and Quinn should be asleep.

Instead she's lying in bed with her phone an inch from her face, thumb flicking through TikTok on autopilot: a cooking video, a dog in sunglasses, someone crying in their car. Swipe. Swipe. Swipe.

Then the screen goes dark red, and a notification slides down from the top:

🔴 RAVEN is LIVE

She taps it without thinking. Everyone does.

The stream opens on a garage lit by neon, red and blue light bouncing off chrome. Raven sits on his motorcycle with his black helmet on, as always. Nobody has ever seen his face. Forty thousand viewers, and the number keeps climbing. Comments pour up the side of the screen faster than anyone could read them.

"RAVEN 🔥🔥🔥"

"take the helmet off coward 😭"

"when's the next race??"

Then the screen splits in two.

TITAN has joined the LIVE.

The comments explode. On the right half of the screen, Titan grins into the camera from a gym, a towel around his neck and his face hidden behind a black balaclava, like always. Only his eyes and his cocky grin show through.

"Still hiding behind that bucket, huh?" Titan says. "What's the matter, Raven? Scared your fans will find out you're ugly?"

"Says the guy who films himself flexing in a mirror for a living," Raven answers. His voice is calm, almost bored, slightly distorted by the helmet. "Do you even know what a real engine sounds like?"

"I know what a clown sounds like."

The comment section turns into a war zone. TEAM TITAN. TEAM RAVEN. FIGHT FIGHT FIGHT. Everybody online knows these two can't stand each other. Every time they're in the same room, digital or not, it ends like this.

Raven tilts his head. "You're boring my viewers."

TITAN has been removed from the LIVE.

Quinn smiles a little in the dark.

Damian.

Everyone at their high school knew Damian. He was the boy who rode a motorcycle into the school parking lot at sixteen, who skipped half his classes and still somehow passed. Half the school was in love with him, and the other half was afraid of him. He was untouchable, the kind of popular that didn't even need to try.

The only one who never treated him that way was Alex, the boy who practically lived in the school gym. Where Damian made everything look effortless, Alex fought for every win, and the two of them turned everything into a competition: races in the parking lot, arm-wrestling at lunch, who got more attention at parties. They were never friends, but never quite enemies either.

She sat two rows behind Damian in senior year. They talked maybe ten times. Once, he caught her staring and winked at her, and she wanted the floor to swallow her whole.

After graduation, everyone went their own way. A few months later, a masked biker called Raven started blowing up online: street races, midnight rides, millions of views overnight. Not long after, a masked fitness star called Titan started blowing up right behind him. The rest of the world had no idea who they were.

But everyone from their old school knew in seconds. The old class group chat exploded. "Bro, that's Damian." "The bike, the attitude, it's literally him." "And Titan is Alex. Obviously."

To millions of strangers, Raven is a mystery. To them, he's just Damian, the boy from the back row who never texted any of them again. She hasn't seen him in person since the last day of school.

Just like that, the screen is whole again. Raven leans toward the camera.

"Now that the noise is gone," he says, "I've got an announcement."

He pauses, letting forty thousand people wait.

"Tonight. Midnight. The old harbor district. No rules, no cops, no limits. Bring your fastest machine." The visor catches the light. "Or just come and watch."

He swings off the bike and walks toward the back wall, taking the phone with him. The camera shakes as he grabs a heavy metal handle and pulls.

Steel screeches. The "wall" swings open, and it's not a wall at all. It's a door.

He was never in a garage. He's been streaming from inside a shipping container.

Cold night air rushes in. Behind him, the camera catches the harbor: rusted cranes against the sky, dark water glittering with city lights, rows of containers stretching into the distance. Standing between them, already waiting, is a crowd of his followers. Dozens of them hold their phones up, their screens glowing like fireflies, and they scream when they see him.

The comment section loses its mind.

"WAIT HE'S ALREADY THERE???"

"NO WAY THAT'S THE HARBOR"

"im omw 🏍️🏍️🏍️"

Raven turns the camera back to his helmet. For a second, the crowd's lights dance across his black visor.

The LIVE has ended.

Quinn stares at the black screen, and her heart is beating a little faster than it should.

An illegal race. Tonight. And Damian, in real life, for the first time in years.

She laughs to herself and locks her phone. As if.

It buzzes immediately.

Mia: DID U SEE THAT

Mia: GIRL. DAMIAN IS DOING A RACE TONIGHT

Mia: we're seeing him IN PERSON

Mia: don't even say no

Mia: we're outside ur house in 15 😈

She types no way, I have stuff to do tomorrow, deletes it, and types it again. She's still holding the phone when a car horn blares twice outside her window.

She pulls the curtain aside. Mia's car is at the curb, music thumping, and three of her friends are leaning out the windows, waving like idiots.

"Come ON!" Mia yells up at her.

She should say no. She knows she should say no.

But it's Damian.

She grabs her jacket.

II. Neon and Smoke

The harbor district is supposed to be dead at night: empty warehouses, rusted cranes and broken streetlights.

Tonight it's alive.

Hundreds of people crowd between the shipping containers. Cars line the streets with their hoods open, engines painted in colors that shouldn't exist: purple chrome, glowing underlights, exhausts that spit blue flame when they rev. Motorcycles weave through the crowd like sharks. Music pounds from somewhere, so loud she feels it in her chest. Phones are everywhere, raised up and filming everything.

"This is insane," Quinn whispers.

"This is amazing," Mia corrects her, already filming.

Then the crowd shifts and goes quiet for a heartbeat. People turn and point.

Far away, at the end of the strip, a lone motorcycle rolls into the light: a black bike with a black helmet.

Raven.

He doesn't wave or speak. He just sits there while the crowd screams his name, his visor turned toward them like a mirror reflecting a thousand lights. For a strange second, she feels like he's looking straight at her.

Don't be stupid. There are hundreds of people here.

She raises her phone and starts recording, slowly panning across the crowd, the neon and the engines. Nobody will believe this without a video.

III. The Exchange

Quinn doesn't notice them. Nobody does. That's the point.

Near a stack of shipping containers, away from the main strip, a man leans against a pillar in a dark, perfectly fitted jacket. He looks relaxed, but his eyes never stop moving: left, right, behind him. Viper is keeping watch.

A few steps away, a man in an oversized hoodie and a face covering waits with his hands in his pockets. Chaos looks almost bored.

A nervous man in a cap pushes through the crowd toward him, clutching a black case to his chest. His hands are shaking.

"You have the money?" the man whispers.

Chaos smiles under his mask. "Relax."

The case changes hands.

On the main strip, the race begins. Twenty engines roar at the same time, a wall of sound so loud the ground shakes, and the crowd screams over it.

Nobody hears the gunshot.

The man in the cap stumbles. He looks down at his chest, confused, like he's trying to understand something simple. Then his legs fold and he collapses between the bodies of the crowd.

In the shadows behind him, a slim figure in black lowers his hand and slips the weapon back under his jacket. Shade doesn't blink. It was clean and quiet, exactly as planned.

For three seconds, nobody notices.

Then someone screams.

IV. The Video

The scream spreads like fire. People shove and run. Someone yells "He's been shot!" and the crowd breaks apart in every direction.

Mia grabs her arm. "We have to go. Now!"

They run, ducking behind a parked car, gasping for air. Quinn's hands are shaking so badly she almost drops her phone, and that's when she realizes it's still recording.

She stops it. Then, without knowing why, she plays it back.

Neon, the crowd, the engines. Then, at the edge of the frame, half-hidden between the containers, a man in a hoodie with a face covering takes a black case from a man in a cap.

Two seconds later, the man in the cap falls.

Her breath stops. She zooms in with trembling fingers. The face is covered, but the eyes are clear: cold, amused, looking almost straight into her camera.

"What is it?" Mia whispers. "What did you film?"

She can't answer. She just shakes her head.

"Let's go," she says. "Please. Let's just go."

V. Seen

Across the chaos, Shade grabs Viper's arm. "Move."

But Viper doesn't move right away. His eyes have locked onto something: a girl crouched behind a car with a phone in her hands, white as a ghost, staring at the screen like it just showed her something she can't forget.

She was facing exactly where Chaos was standing.

"Viper." Shade's voice is low and sharp. "Now."

Viper tears his eyes away. Chaos is already beside them with the case under his arm. The three of them vanish into the dark between the containers, fast and silent, as if they were never there.

VI. The Reflection

At the end of the strip, one motorcycle hasn't moved.

Raven sits on his bike, perfectly still, while the world falls apart around him. Sirens wail somewhere in the distance. People run past him on both sides.

He's watching one car.

The girl climbs into the back seat, her friends piling in after her, doors slamming, tires screeching as they pull away.

He knows that face.

The same eyes, the same way of biting her lip when she's scared. It's been years since he last saw her in the hallways at school. She probably thinks he forgot her a long time ago.

But he didn't forget her.

And he knows, the way you just know things, that she saw something tonight that she was never supposed to see.

The car's headlights sweep across his helmet. For one moment, her escape is reflected in his black visor: the car, the neon and the night, swallowed by the dark.

— BLACKOUT —

VII. The Morning After

Quinn barely sleeps.

When she finally opens her eyes, the sun is already high, and her phone is buzzing nonstop. She doesn't want to look at it.

She looks anyway.

The murder at the harbor is everywhere: on the news, on every account and in every comment section.

MAN SHOT DEAD AT ILLEGAL STREET RACE ORGANIZED BY INFLUENCER 'RAVEN'

Police: victim identified as a former soldier. No suspects yet.

Her For You page is full of it. Shaky crowd videos from a hundred different angles. People crying, people joking, people pretending they were there. Conspiracy theories in green-screen videos with red arrows.

Then a video from Titan.

"So let me get this straight," he says, leaning toward the camera, smirking. "Mr. Mystery Helmet throws a party, and somebody ends up dead? Come on. Everybody knows what that was. A drug deal gone wrong, and our boy Raven was right in the middle of it. I'm just saying what everyone's thinking." He shrugs. "Notice how quiet he's been?"

The comments agree. He hasn't posted since. No live, no story, nothing. Raven's hiding something. Where is he??

Raven's profile is silent. His last post is still the announcement from last night.

She closes the app and puts the phone face-down on her bed. She doesn't open the video in her gallery. She doesn't want to see those eyes again.

It's over. You were never there. Nobody knows.

She texts Mia: can i come to your home tonight? I don't want to be alone.

VIII. Do You Remember Me?

The evening is quiet and normal, and that's exactly what she needs.

Quinn and Mia walk back from her house under the streetlights, and for the first time all day her shoulders relax. Maybe it really is over.

Then she hears an engine.

Low and slow, it rolls up behind them.

A motorcycle stops at the curb beside them. A black bike. A black helmet. The visor reflects the two girls standing frozen on the sidewalk.

Mia grabs her hand. "Is that…?"

The rider doesn't take off his helmet. He just turns his head toward her, only her, and for a long moment nobody speaks.

Then his voice comes through the helmet, low and familiar in a way she can't explain.

"Do you remember me?"

Chapter 1

Six Strangers

I. Old Classmates

For a moment, nobody moves.

The engine idles beneath him, low and patient. The streetlight above them flickers. Mia's fingers are still locked around Quinn's hand, so tight it almost hurts.

Then the visor tilts, just slightly, toward the empty seat behind him.

"Get on."

Mia lets out a sound that's half laugh, half panic. "Excuse me? She's not getting on anything with—"

"I'm not asking you." He doesn't even look at Mia. His helmet stays turned toward Quinn, steady and unreadable. "Five minutes. I just want to talk."

Every reasonable part of her brain is screaming. Last night a man died at his race. Nobody knows what he's involved in. You don't even know what he wants.

But this is Damian, the boy from two rows ahead, the one who winked at her once and then disappeared into a life that millions of people watch through a screen.

"Quinn," Mia hisses. "Don't you dare."

He knows her name. Somewhere under the fear, that small fact lands harder than it should.

"I'll bring her back in one piece," Raven says, and she can hear the smirk in his voice. "Promise."

Quinn lets go of Mia's hand.

"Text me," Mia says, eyes wide, already pulling out her phone. "Text me EVERY minute. I'm serious. I'll call the police. I'll call your mom."

Quinn swings her leg over the seat. The leather is cold. She hesitates, not sure where to put her hands, until he reaches back, takes her wrists and pulls her arms around his waist.

"Hold on," he says. "Tight."

The engine roars, and the street disappears behind them.

The city blurs past in streaks of light: red, gold, the neon blue of a pharmacy sign, the white flash of headlights. The wind tears at her hair, and she presses her face against his back without meaning to. He smells like leather, rain and engine smoke. He drives fast, but never carelessly, weaving through traffic like the road was built just for him.

For a few minutes, she forgets to be afraid.

He takes them up a spiral ramp to the top floor of an empty parking garage. The whole city is spread out below them: skyscrapers, bridges, and far away, the dark shape of the harbor, where blue police lights still flash between the containers.

He kills the engine. The sudden silence rings in her ears.

Quinn climbs off first, legs shaking a little. Raven stays on the bike for a moment, then swings off and leans back against it, arms crossed. The helmet stays on.

"You're not going to take that off?" she asks.

"Would you like me to?"

"I already know it's you."

"Then you don't need to see it." He tilts his head, that same slow, lazy angle she recognized through her phone screen. "You grew up, Harrison."

Her cheeks burn. "That's what people do."

"Not everyone." A pause. "You still bite your lip when you're nervous."

She stops biting her lip immediately. He laughs, a low, quiet sound, and for a second, it's almost easy, like they're just two old classmates bumping into each other after years.

Then his voice changes. It gets softer, and careful.

"You were at the race last night." It's not a question. "I saw you leaving. Are you okay?"

She wasn't ready for that. The concern sounds real, and something tight in her chest loosens a little.

"I'm fine," she says. "I mean… I'm not. A man died, Damian."

"I know." He looks out at the harbor lights. "It wasn't supposed to happen like that."

Like that. The words snag in her head, but before she can ask, he turns back to her.

"You looked pretty shaken up," he says lightly. "Running to that car with your phone in your hand. Everyone was filming last night, huh?"

Her fingers tighten around the phone in her jacket pocket.

"I guess."

"Did you get anything good?" Still light, still casual. "The race, the crowd… anything?"

There it is.

She suddenly understands why he came, why he waited for her and why he took her up here, away from Mia, away from everyone.

He isn't here to check on her. He's here for the video.

"No," she says. Her voice comes out steadier than she expects. "I didn't film anything. I was too scared."

The silence stretches. She can't see his face, only her own reflection in the black visor: small, pale and lying.

"Okay," he says finally.

He doesn't believe her. She can feel it.

He pushes himself off the bike and steps closer, close enough that she has to tilt her head back to look up at the helmet. He holds out his hand.

"Give me your phone."

Her heart stops. "Why?"

"Relax." Amusement creeps back into his voice. "I'm giving you my number."

She hesitates one second too long. He notices. Of course he notices.

Slowly, she hands it over, praying he doesn't open her gallery.

He doesn't. He types something quickly, his thumbs gliding over the screen, and hands it back. A new contact glows on the display:

Raven 🖤

"In case you remember something," he says. "Or in case you need help. Last night made a lot of people nervous, Quinn. Nervous people do stupid things."

"Is that a warning?"

"It's advice." He turns back to the bike and swings his leg over. "I'll take you back."

He drops her at the corner of Mia's street, exactly where he found her. Mia is still standing under the streetlight, phone in her hand, looking like she hasn't breathed in twenty minutes.

Quinn climbs off. Before she can say anything, Raven revs the engine once and leans toward her.

"Call me," he says. "Before someone else finds you first."

Then he's gone, a red taillight shrinking into the dark until the night swallows it whole.

Quinn stands there, her phone heavy in her pocket, like it's suddenly made of stone.

He knows.

II. The Black Veil

Behind a boxing gym that closed hours ago, a single bulb swings over a concrete room that smells of sweat and old blood.

The black case sits on a metal table.

Next to it sits Silas Vale, all six-foot-five of him, shoulders as wide as the doorway, hood pulled back and mask hanging around his thick neck. A cigarette hangs from his lips, and he keeps flicking a cheap lighter that refuses to catch. Click. Click. Click.

In the corner, Marco can't stop talking. He was supposed to watch the crowd last night.

"It was clean, I swear. Nobody saw nothing. And if anyone messed up, it was James. He was the lookout, not me…"

Click.

The lighter stops.

Silas stands up. The table scrapes across the floor. In three steps he crosses the room, and Marco barely has time to raise his hands before a fist the size of a brick drives him into the wall. Concrete cracks. The bulb swings wildly, throwing shadows everywhere.

"Say his name again." Silas lifts him off the ground by the collar like he weighs nothing. "Go on."

Marco chokes out something that might be sorry.

Silas smiles. "Wrong answer."

One brutal motion, and Marco drops to the floor and doesn't get up again.

Silas rolls his shoulders, cracks his neck, and sits back on the table as if he's just taken out the trash. The unlit cigarette is still between his lips. Click. Click.

"Nobody blames James," he says to the empty air. "Nobody."

James Calloway hasn't moved from his spot by the door. He stands with his hands in the pockets of his tailored jacket, perfectly calm, like none of this has anything to do with him. Only his jaw is tight.

"You didn't have to do that," he says quietly.

"Yeah, I did." Silas grins. "Nobody talks about you like that. Not while I'm breathing."

James sighs. He's heard it a hundred times, and somehow it still means something.

In the shadows at the back of the room, a third figure leans against the wall, dressed in black from head to toe, mask covering everything but his eyes. He hasn't said a single word since he arrived. Most people in this world don't even know his real name. They just call him Shade.

"So," Silas says, tapping the black case with the lighter. "We have it. The seller won't be talking to anyone. The biker boy kept everyone busy with his little show, exactly like we paid him to." He spreads his arms. "Perfect night. Why do you look like someone died, James?" A beat, then he laughs at his own joke. "Okay. Besides the obvious."

James doesn't laugh.

"There might be a problem."

The lighter stops. "I hate problems."

"There was a girl." James chooses his words carefully. "During the exchange. She was standing behind a car with her phone up. When everything happened, she was looking at the screen like… like she'd just seen something she shouldn't have." He pauses. "She was facing exactly where you were standing, Silas."

The silence that follows feels heavy.

Then Silas bursts out laughing, loud and genuinely delighted.

"A girl? With a phone?" He wipes his eye. "James. Half the city was filming last night. You know how many shaky videos of neon lights and screaming idiots are online right now? Thousands."

"Maybe. But she wasn't filming the race." James meets his eyes. "She was filming you."

Silas's smile stays, but something behind it goes cold.

He hops off the table and walks to the back of the room, where Shade is standing. Silas is the only one in the city who would stand that close to him.

"Hear that?" he says. "Some little girl might have me on her phone. Taking a case from a dead man."

Shade says nothing. His eyes don't even move.

"Find her," Silas says, his voice suddenly flat and quiet. "Find out who she is, where she lives, who she talks to." He leans in. "And when you find her… Shade, you finish it."

For a moment, Shade doesn't react. Then he gives one slow nod.

"Clean," Silas adds. "Like last night."

Shade pushes off the wall and walks out without a sound. The door doesn't even creak behind him.

Click.

This time, the flame catches. Silas lights his cigarette, takes a long drag and blows the smoke toward the ceiling, the grin already returning, as if he just ordered takeout instead of a death.

"Relax, James. By the end of the week, nobody will even remember her name."

James doesn't answer. He looks down at the black case on the table, then at the door Shade just walked through.

He thinks about the girl's face, white as a ghost, frozen behind that car. She didn't look like a threat. She looked like someone who'd just watched her whole life change.

He says nothing.

But for the first time in a long time, James Calloway isn't sure he's on the right side.

III. The Soldier

The morgue is always cold. Evan Barrett doesn't notice anymore.

He stands over the steel table in silence, a black cap pulled low and a half-face mask covering everything below his eyes. The sheet is folded down to the dead man's chest.

Daniel Price. Thirty-four. Gunshot wound, one shot, close range.

"I'm sorry," says the woman behind him. Lieutenant Hayes has known Evan long enough not to touch his shoulder. "I know he was one of yours."

"He was the last one," Evan says quietly.

First Marcus, on the mission that went wrong. Now Daniel, on a wet street by the harbor, in the middle of a party.

Evan pulls the sheet back over his friend's face. His hands are perfectly steady. They always are.

"Why was he there?"

Hayes hesitates. Then she holds out a thin folder. "Off the record? He reached out to us a week ago. Said he had something big. Evidence on a black case, connected to an old cover-up. Someone higher up buried it, and he was going to sell it to the right people." She looks at the body. "Looks like the wrong people found him first."

"And the case?"

"Gone." Hayes lowers her voice. "And officially, nobody is looking for it. Orders came down fast this morning. Close the file, call it a drug deal gone wrong."

Evan's eyes come up from the table. Grey, cold, unreadable.

"But you're here."

"I'm here." She presses the folder into his hands. "Find out who took it. Get the evidence before it disappears for good. Quietly, Barrett. No noise, no badges, no backup. If anyone asks, I never saw you."

He doesn't thank her. He just nods.

In his car, rain streaks across the windshield. Evan opens the laptop from the folder. It's full of crowd videos, dozens of them, pulled from TikTok and Instagram: neon, engines, screaming people, and a hundred shaking phones.

He watches them all, again and again, frame by frame, the way he was trained to.

On the fourteenth video, he stops.

The edge of the frame shows the containers and a hooded figure taking something black from a man in a cap. And just across from them, half-hidden behind a parked car, is a girl. She's young, pale and frozen, and her phone is raised and pointed at exactly the right spot.

Evan zooms in until her face fills the screen.

She's not a criminal or a cop, and she isn't part of any of this. She's just a girl who was in the wrong place at the wrong time.

And right now, she's the only witness in the city.

"Found you," he murmurs.

He pulls his cap lower and starts the engine.
